3 Minutes Before the Meeting · Briefing 05 · Under three minutes to read · November 2026

Is our school’s technology safe, secure and fit for purpose?

The Department for Education has set digital and technology standards for schools. Every school should be working towards six core standards by 2030: broadband internet, wireless network, network switching, digital leadership and governance, filtering and monitoring, and cyber security.

Two of these cannot wait. Keeping children safe online is already a statutory responsibility, and the filtering and monitoring and cyber security standards are the DfE's recommended way of meeting it. Governing bodies hold overall strategic responsibility for filtering and monitoring.

Governors do not need to be technical. Their responsibility is to seek assurance that named people are in charge, that checks are done on time and that the school could recover from an attack.

Look for

  • Named people: A senior leader responsible for digital technology, a governor responsible for filtering and monitoring, and at least one governor or trustee who has had cyber security training.
  • Checks done on time: A cyber risk assessment every year, reviewed each term. Filtering and monitoring reviewed at least once every academic year, with the checks recorded. Backups tested each term.
  • A plan that has been tested: A cyber response plan within the business continuity plan, a recovery plan tested at least once a year, and incidents reported to the board.

Three questions

  1. Who is our senior lead for digital technology, which governor is responsible for filtering and monitoring, and what did the last annual review find?
  2. When was our cyber risk assessment last carried out, what were the highest risks, and what has been done about them?
  3. If we lost our systems tomorrow, how long could we keep running, when were our backups and recovery plan last tested, and who would we report the attack to?

Send to your board

One thing to follow up

Ask leaders for a one-page position against the six core standards, showing for each:

Met · Partly met · Not met · The plan and cost to 2030

Governors should seek evidence and hold leaders to account, not choose filters, manage systems or handle incidents themselves.

Remember: An IT contract or a policy is not evidence that systems are safe. Ask what was tested, when, and what was found.
