3 Minutes Before the Meeting · Briefing 04 · Under three minutes to read · November 2026

Are pupils with SEND getting the right support – and how do we know?

Every class includes pupils with special educational needs or disabilities (SEND). Most are taught in mainstream classrooms, where everyday teaching matters as much as specialist support.

Ofsted now grades inclusion in its own right. It covers pupils with SEND, disadvantaged pupils, pupils known to children's social care and others who face barriers to learning. National SEND reform is proposed, but until the law changes the existing duties apply.

Governors and trustees do not decide what support a child receives. The board must use its best endeavours to see that pupils with SEND get the support they need, make sure the school has a qualified SENCo and publish an SEN information report. Its role is to hold leaders to account for how well this works.

Look for

  • Early identification: Needs are spotted quickly and accurately, and families are involved early in decisions about support.
  • Everyday provision: The support expected in every mainstream classroom is in place and reviewed. Many local authorities set this out as "ordinarily available" guidance; in Birmingham, the Ordinarily Available Guidance (OAG).
  • Impact by group: Attendance, suspensions and progress for pupils with SEND and disadvantaged pupils, compared with their peers.

Three questions

  1. How quickly are pupils' needs identified, and what do parents and pupils tell us about the support they receive?
  2. How do leaders know that the support expected in every classroom is in place, and that the SENCo has the time and authority to lead it?
  3. What do attendance, suspensions and progress show for pupils with SEND and disadvantaged pupils compared with their peers, and what has changed as a result?

Send to your board

One thing to follow up

Ofsted's toolkit expects leaders, with governors or trustees, to publish an inclusion strategy. In a trust, ask which trustee or committee oversees it. Read it with the SEN information report and ask:

Is it accurate · Is it up to date · Does it show how funding meets our pupils' needs · Would parents recognise it

Remember: A well-written SEND policy does not show that pupils are well supported. Look for evidence of what pupils experience and achieve, without asking for information that identifies individual children.
