3 Minutes Before the Meeting · Briefing 02 · Under three minutes to read · November 2026

Is our school using AI safely?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly part of school life. Staff may use it to prepare lessons, create resources or support administration. Pupils may also encounter AI through apps and online platforms.

AI can be useful, but it brings safeguarding, privacy and accountability risks. These include sharing personal information, inaccurate AI-generated content and harmful or manipulated images.

Governors do not need to be AI experts. Their responsibility is to seek assurance that school leaders understand the risks, have appropriate safeguards and know whether those safeguards are working.

Look for

  • Clear oversight: Leaders know which AI tools are being used, for what purposes and who is responsible for approving them.
  • Safeguarding and privacy: Staff understand what information must not be entered into unapproved tools, and existing safeguarding procedures can respond to AI-related harm.
  • Evidence of practice: The school can show relevant checks, staff guidance, training and actions taken – not simply an AI policy.

Three questions

  1. What AI tools are being used in our school, and how do leaders know they are safe and appropriate for their intended use?
  2. How do we know staff understand the safeguarding and data protection risks, including what to do if an AI-related concern arises?
  3. What evidence shows our AI safeguards are working, and what gaps still need addressing?

Send to your board

One thing to follow up

Ask leaders to provide a short update at the next appropriate meeting covering:

What is approved · What has been checked · What needs action · Who is responsible

Governors should seek evidence and hold leaders to account, not select AI tools or manage incidents themselves.

Remember: Having an AI policy is not the same as knowing that AI is being used safely.
