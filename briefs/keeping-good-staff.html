3 Minutes Before the Meeting · Briefing 03 · Under three minutes to read · November 2026

Are we keeping good staff – or simply replacing them?

From Information to Assurance.

Schools depend on skilled, motivated teachers, leaders and support staff. But excessive workload, stress and staffing pressures can affect wellbeing, recruitment and retention.

Education Support's Teacher Wellbeing Index 2025 reported that 76% of surveyed education staff experienced stress. This highlights why staff wellbeing and workforce sustainability deserve attention at governing board level.

Governors are not responsible for managing individual staff. Their role is to ensure that school leaders understand workforce pressures, listen to staff and take meaningful action.

Look for

  • Staff voice: Evidence that leaders listen to teaching and support staff about workload and wellbeing, and respond to their concerns.
  • Workforce patterns: Information about staff turnover, vacancies, sickness absence and recruitment difficulties, considered in context.
  • Action and impact: Examples of unnecessary workload being reduced, working practices improved and evidence that these changes are helping.

Three questions

  1. What are staff telling us about workload and wellbeing, and how do we know leaders are listening and responding?
  2. What do staff absence, recruitment and retention patterns tell us about the sustainability of our workforce?
  3. What practical changes have leaders made to reduce unnecessary workload, and how do we know they are working?

One thing to follow up

At the next appropriate meeting, ask for a short, anonymised update showing:

What staff told us · What leaders changed · What difference it made · What still needs attention

Remember: Having a staff wellbeing policy does not necessarily mean staff are experiencing a healthy working culture. Governors should seek evidence of improvement, not simply a list of wellbeing activities.
